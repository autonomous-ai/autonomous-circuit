# The ideal users — who's creating, and who they're creating for

**Load when:** starting a project from a vague prompt, deciding what to ask the
user vs. what to infer, choosing defaults, or writing any text the creator reads.
The platform serves **two** people at once: the **creator** (a non-producer
typing to us) and the **viewer** (the hooked audience the series must hook).
The creator brings passion and taste; the tool supplies everything else — and it
designs for the *viewer* on the creator's behalf, because the creator usually
can't name their own audience.

## Part A — the creator (who's typing)

Not a producer. Can't storyboard, doesn't know "cliffhanger cadence" or "9:16
coverage," and shouldn't have to. They arrive with a *feeling* and a fandom.

**Personas (design for these, not for a film-school grad):**
- **The binge-watcher mom** (the core). 35-55+, watches ReelShort/DramaBox nightly,
  knows exactly what she loves (CEO romance, revenge, werewolf) but has never made
  anything. Wants: "make me one like the ones I can't stop watching." Speaks in
  *vibes and references* ("like "The CEO's Secret Wife" but with a nurse").
- **The fanfic / Wattpad writer.** Has characters and a world they're obsessed
  with; strong on story, zero on production. Wants their ship on screen. This is
  the highest-retention creator — serialized passion is their native mode.
- **The student in Brazil / SEA / anywhere.** Mobile-first, price-sensitive,
  wants a shot at the thing they see going viral. Local taste (telenovela-revenge,
  family drama). Needs it *cheap and in their language/market*.
- **The hustler.** Sees the money in short drama, wants volume and ad-hooks to run
  traffic. Cares about what converts, not craft. Serve them the 投流表.

**What they CAN articulate:** a vibe, a fantasy, a genre they love, characters
and a relationship, "make her the underdog," "I want him to grovel." Emotional
wants, in plain words.
**What they CANNOT (and must never be asked):** beat cadence, shot grammar,
paywall episode number, reversal density, act structure, resolution/exposition
budgets. The binge machine is *ours* to run (`binge-engine.md`).

**So how the tool talks to them:**
- **No jargon, ever.** "Who should we root for, and what got taken from them?"
  not "define the protagonist's ghost/wound." Translate every craft term into a
  feeling question.
- **Ask 2-3 taste questions, infer the rest.** The minimal set: *who's the fantasy
  for (and what do they wish would happen to them)? what's the wound / injustice?
  what do they want more than anything?* From those, pick the genre engine
  (`genre-playbook.md`), plant the ep-1 wound, and schedule the loop. Don't
  interrogate; propose and let them react.
- **Show the story taking shape; let them steer by feel.** They react to a draft
  far better than they spec from scratch — "make her angrier here," "I want the
  sister to be the villain." Give them the reaction surface, not a form.
- **Default aggressively; make everything reversible.** A non-producer freezes on
  a blank page. Fill it (genre, cast, beats, gate) and invite edits. A good default
  they tweak beats a hard question they can't answer.

**Creator failure modes to catch (and gently fix):**
- **Over-specifying plot, under-specifying feeling** — they'll dump events; we ask
  "and how should that make her feel? who's watching it happen?" to find the 爽点.
- **"Just make it good"** — too vague to act on; offer 2-3 concrete directions
  (revenge vs. romance framing) and let them pick.
- **Copying a hit's surface without the engine** — they want "the same but mine";
  give them the *engine* under the hit (the loop), reskinned, not a plot Xerox.

## Part B — the viewer (who it's for)

The creator is making it for someone specific, and that someone is *not the
creator*. The tool holds the viewer model and designs to it. (Full audience study:
`docs/short-drama-playbook.md` Part 1; genre→audience map in `genre-playbook.md`.)

**Persona shorthands the tool designs for:**
- **The nightly binge-watcher** (35-55+, skews female, suburban/lower-income; the
  soap/Hallmark audience on a phone). Watches before bed, often on mute, half-
  attentively. Wants: to be *seen, cherished, vindicated*. Genres: CEO/billionaire,
  werewolf/fated-mate, revenge, in-law. **The largest payer pool** (older women
  convert and pay unusually well).
- **The power-fantasy viewer** (male-skew): wants respect/dominance/the-dismissed-
  nobody-revealed. Genres: 战神/赘婿, hidden-identity, sudden-wealth.
- **The regional family-drama viewer** (SEA/LATAM/India): collectivist, family-
  centric, telenovela DNA. Genres: family/in-law, revenge+family-honor.

**What every viewer needs from the frame (design invariants):** a 3-second hook
(they're one thumb-flick from gone); muted legibility (captions carry it —
`emotion-to-action.md`); a self-insert to *be*; a payoff every 60-90s; a
cliffhanger so the next tap is the only relief. These aren't creator choices —
they're viewer requirements the tool enforces regardless of what the creator
knows.

## The bridge (the whole job in one line)

**The creator brings a fantasy in plain words; the tool maps it to a viewer + a
genre engine + the binge machine, and hands back an bingeable series they
steer by feel.** They never see the machinery. That mapping — feeling → viewer →
engine → loop — is what makes "anyone can create an bingeable series by chatting"
literally true.

## Pitfalls

- **Talking to the creator like a filmmaker.** Jargon makes a non-producer bounce.
  Every question is a feeling question.
- **Designing for the creator's taste instead of the viewer's.** The creator's job
  is passion + steering; the *viewer's* wants set the structure. When they conflict
  (the creator wants a slow, arty open), serve the viewer and show why.
- **A blank page.** Never ask an open "what do you want to make?" and stop. Propose.
- **Ignoring market.** A Brazilian student's hit is not a US mom's hit — localize
  the engine (`genre-playbook.md` "localization is rewrite").
