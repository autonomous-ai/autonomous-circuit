# Master shot craft — great-film technique that survives 90s vertical

**Load when:** writing/fixing shot prompts, when the board looks flat or "AI",
when a turn/reveal/gut-punch doesn't land, or when a scene reads unclear on a
phone. This is master-director technique filtered to what survives a 6" 9:16
screen and a keyframe→i2v pipeline. Tags: **[K]** keyframe (image prompt) · **[M]**
motion (i2v prompt) · **[S]** scene/edit logic. Pairs with `cinematic-shots.md`
(shot vocabulary) and `shot-grammar.md` (durations/scales).

## Tension & the turn
- **[K] Put the threat the character can't see IN the keyframe** (Hitchcock's
  bomb-under-the-table): the text on the phone screen, the figure in the doorway,
  the ring on another woman's hand — the audience sees it, the character doesn't.
  Dread is the entertainment; the vertical frame can hold a secret behind an
  oblivious face for free.
- **[S] One shot = one value change** (safe→unsafe, loved→betrayed, winning→
  losing). If a shot doesn't flip something, cut it.
- **[S] Land the turn on a single gesture + a single line** — the smile drops, the
  hand freezes, the eyes cut off-camera. Not a paragraph ("I know it was you,
  Fredo" + the kiss).
- **[K][S] Coil the spring before the turn:** tighten framing shot-over-shot —
  medium → face → eyes/hand (Leone's standoff).
- **[K] A deadline gets a clock/countdown object in frame** (High Noon).
- **[S] Compressed Chekhov:** plant a prop in one keyframe, fire it within 3-4 shots.

## The reveal
- **[S] Reveal to the AUDIENCE before the character;** hold on the oblivious face
  to milk the dread.
- **[S] The reaction shot beats the thing** (Kuleshov / the "Spielberg face"):
  often hold the watching face and let the viewer fill in — cheaper and stronger.
- **[M] On a realization beat, push in slowly on the face and hold ~1s past the
  line.**
- **[M] Stage the reveal as a move:** rack focus foreground→face, a body stepping
  aside to expose what was blocked, or a whip-pan landing on the new subject.
- **[K] Prop reveal:** keyframe a hand placing the object (divorce papers, a
  paternity test, a photo), then cut to the reaction.
- **[K] Withhold:** keep the crucial element in shadow or just out of frame, reveal
  it next shot (Jaws' shark).

## Blocking & power (reads instantly)
- **[K] Power = low angle on the dominant character; weakness = high angle on the
  diminished one** (Welles/Kurosawa).
- **[K] Standing beats sitting** — flip power by having one character rise while
  the other stays seated.
- **[M] Block on the Z-AXIS (toward/away), never side-to-side.** The vertical frame
  is narrow horizontally, so lateral crosses read as nothing; depth reads huge —
  approach = aggression/intimacy, retreat = fear/submission.
- **[M] Push in for emotion** (slow = dawning realization, fast = shock); **whip-pan
  for reveals** and to hide a cut.
- **[K] Dutch-tilt only on the beat the moral ground shifts;** level otherwise.

## Vertical composition
- **[K] Default to a face-filling close-up;** eyes on the upper-third line; keep
  load-bearing action in the center vertical strip (phone-safe).
- **[K] Never rely on two readable faces at once** — stack them (one above/behind
  the other) or use singles in shot-reverse-shot.
- **[K] Stack story vertically:** an object/shoulder low in frame, the face above
  (a vertical over-the-shoulder).
- **[K] Use the tall frame's vertical lines** — doorways, corridors, staircases;
  descending a staircase = falling status.
- **[K] Negative space above the head = isolation;** filling the frame edge-to-edge
  = claustrophobia.
- **[K][M] Broad, legible gestures — no micro-expressions;** the story must read at
  6 inches.

## The gut-punch
- **[S] Give the emotion an object early, and END the beat on that object** — the
  empty chair, the unanswered phone, the ring left behind (Up, Toy Story 3).
- **[M] After the emotional hit, HOLD the still face 1-2s with no cut** — the
  contrast against fast cutting is the punch.
- **[S] Cut on the wound** (the flinch, the breaking voice), not after it.
- **[M] Slow-motion only on the single emotional glance/gesture** (Wong Kar-wai).

## Light & color (keyframe-locked — highest leverage)
- **[K] Light every keyframe from ONE motivated source** (window, lamp, candle,
  phone screen, TV) — name the source and its direction (Deakins; Rembrandt).
- **[K] Eyes in shadow = hidden intent/threat** (Willis on the Godfather).
- **[K] One grade per emotional chapter:** amber = safety/romance/memory · teal =
  lonely/cold/night · red = danger/passion/desire · sickly green = dread/envy.
- **[K][S] Shift the grade warm→cold across the episode when the relationship turns.**
- **[K] Low-key/dark = tension; high-key/bright = safe/comic.** Underlight villains,
  toplight the isolated, soft-light the vulnerable, silhouette the doorway entrance;
  practical neon/lamps give night-city depth in vertical.

## Anti-rules (what does NOT transfer — and the substitute)
- **[S] No wide establishing masters** — establish with one detail insert (a
  nameplate, a skyline slice, the defining object) or a tight single with a context
  clue.
- **[S] No ambiguity / open endings** — on a phone that reads as "nothing
  happened." Make subtext text; end on a clear cliffhanger question. Melodrama, not
  art film.
- **[S] Max 2 people per shot;** represent a group as one antagonist + reaction shots.
- **[K] No deep-focus clutter** — shallow focus, isolate the subject, rack focus to
  redirect attention.
- **[S] Don't wait on a score to build** — a sound-design stab + a beat of silence
  on a held frame carries it (`sound-design.md`).
