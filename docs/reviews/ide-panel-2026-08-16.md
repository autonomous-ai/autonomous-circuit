## What the round changed

Every must-fix from lenses 1, 3, 4, 5 and 6 was fixed the same day, each with a
test that fails without it. In commit order:

- `d9f62f1` — the gate is wired into every geometry edit, carrying the moves the
  file is holding, and its answer (including what it did **not** check) is on
  screen. Lens 4's whole finding.
- `377c7d2` — four taps of the turn key are four turns (the step travels, not
  the angle, and the compare-and-swap token is the written text rather than the
  rendered one), and the key list has a visible way in.
- `a36cff2` — undo refuses to overwrite an edit it did not make, coordinates are
  bounded at ±1000mm, `board_source_write` joins the per-project edit queue.
- `5dd4e7f` — `+`/`−` answer the keys they were already printed on, and
  Shift+wheel travels sideways.
- `86a0a8a`, `dc9b3ea` — the gate's own latency: 6,332ms → 578ms on
  harness-puck, and a correction in public when the first attempt at that quietly
  dropped the courtyard check.
- `a0523ff` — the parts lock had never once reached a BOM row: two silent breaks
  in a row, the file's shape and the match key.
- `f24a5e7` — the turn buttons say why clockwise writes 270.

Two of round 1's findings are still open on purpose, both recorded rather than
patched:

- **A turn gets no legality check**, because the gate translates elements and
  cannot rotate them. Pending turns are counted and printed as *not covered*
  rather than folded silently into a green chip. Closing it properly is
  rotation-aware predicted geometry in `fastcheck.apply_moves`.
- **The two boards with no parts lock stay without one.** Writing the lock fills
  the BOM's Footprint column and takes `fab.ready` from true to false — ledger
  #46, reverted whole under the floor rule.

## The bar, and where the round left it

The ship bar is every lens ≥ 7, Familiarity and Editing ≥ 8, zero must-fix, and
each board ≤ 3 human edits that are all judgement calls. Round 1 met none of the
four. Round 2 runs against `dc9b3ea` and re-scores every lens on the same
rubric, with each judge told what was claimed fixed and asked to verify it
rather than believe it.
