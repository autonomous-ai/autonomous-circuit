# The first human EE review — terminal-keyboard, 2026-08-15

The review packet asked our EE team one question: *would you let a non-engineer
use this unsupervised and order the boards it produces?* The first expert
answer came back on terminal-keyboard, and it is the most valuable artifact
this project has received. Verdict, translated:

> "Solid basic prototype — good enough that a non-specialist can produce a
> fairly complete PCB design. **Not manufacturable yet, because it would not
> work.**"

That sentence is the two-bar doctrine (`north-star.md`) spoken back to us by
someone who had never read it: `fab.ready` passed, and the board still fails
the bar that matters. Every finding below is recorded the way Dee directed:
**not as a terminal-keyboard fix, but as the generic defect class, verified by
measurement, with the structural fix that protects every future board.**

---

## Finding 1 — "Every switch in the key matrix is shorted"

**What the EE saw:** in the exported KiCad schematic, each key renders as a
2-pin switch symbol with a wire looping from pin 1 to pin 2 — the two contacts
tied together. As drawn, pressing the button does nothing. All 50 keys.

**What we measured:** the copper is (almost certainly) correct; the schematic
is what lies.

- `sw-tact` ties pins 1+2 → signal, 3+4 → row (`sw-tact.tsx` TR_p1..p4),
  betting on the {1,2}/{3,4} internal pairing. `BLOCK.md` flagged this exact
  bet: *"if the real pairing is 1+3/2+4 … every board using it is scrap."*
- LCSC's own symbol for C318884 (EasyEDA API, fetched 2026-08-15) draws
  internal bars joining 1–2 and 3–4, with the actuator between the rows —
  the pairing our block assumed. Copper: correct under that pairing.
- But the KiCad export renders the 4-pin button as a **2-pin symbol**, so the
  redundant same-net tie becomes a visible short across the switch. The EE
  reviewed the artifact honestly; the artifact misrepresents the board.

**Generic defect class: a review artifact that misrepresents the design.**
A reviewer can only see what the export shows. If the schematic lies, expert
review is spent debunking phantoms — or worse, trusting phantoms. This is the
same family as the sidecar-vs-rebuild gap: the artifact of record must be
derived truthfully or it is worse than nothing.

**Structural fixes:**
1. Schematic conversion must render the pushbutton with its true pin count
   and internal pairing (or fold redundant same-net ties into the symbol
   instead of drawing them as wires).
2. `sw-tact` should wire **diagonally** (signal → 1, to → 4) so the block is
   correct under either side-pairing and the redundancy bet disappears.
3. First-article continuity check stays mandatory (`BLOCK.md` already says
   so) — the LCSC symbol is strong evidence, not a measurement.

## Finding 2 — routing is "better than before, still messy"

Known; matches our own measurements (router at convergence ceiling,
`pipeline-v2.md`). No new action beyond v2's route stage.

## Finding 3 — USB D+/D− must be a differential pair

**Generic class:** constraint-class routing. D+/D− must run parallel, constant
gap, over a ground reference. Our router has no concept of net classes at all
— this is `docs/lessons.md` open item #12 (pair skew), now confirmed by an
expert as a blocker class, not a nicety. **Structural fix:** the v2 route
stage's "critical nets" pass; until then, the check must at least *report*
diff-pair violations on every board (it measures skew today, not coupling).

## Finding 4 — power traces at signal width

5V and 3V3 run at 0.2mm, same as signals. Works for a low-current keyboard;
wrong as a habit. **We already own this number**: `circuitlib` computes
per-net width from current (ledger #13), but the keyboard predates the fix
and nothing re-checks width *class* — a power net at signal width should warn
even when the current maths squeaks by. **Structural fix:** net-class
minimums in the fab profile (power ≥ 0.5mm default), checked at stage 4.

## Finding 5 — 112×90mm board misses the JLC subsidy by 12mm

Under 100×100mm, 5 sample boards cost **$2**; ours quote **$8.90**. The
planner never knew the price cliff existed. **Generic class: fab economics as
plan-time constraints.** The fab profile knows trace floors but not price
tiers.

*Closed on the board too, 2026-08-16.* The 12mm was never circuit: the switch
pads spanned 97.00mm and all copper 99.00mm, while the M2.5 drills spanned
107.70mm — six screws parked outboard of the keycaps. The board is now
**100 × 90**, the screws moved to the top and bottom rails, and the same
rebuild went from **12 blocking findings to 1**. The printed body changes with
it; the numbers it needs are in `examples/terminal-keyboard/boards/main.tsx`
and the README's *What the printed body has to change*.

**Structural fix:** price-tier table in the fab profile
(`sample_subsidy_max_mm: 100`), a planner target ("fit 100×100 unless the
design cannot"), and a check that names the money when a board exceeds a tier
("12mm over the $2 tier — this board costs 4.5× to sample").

## Finding 6 — BOM/CPL not in JLC's own format; designators not grouped

JLC documents an exact sample format; same-part rows should group
(SW1,SW2,…SW50 = one line). Ours emits one row per part. **Generic class:
export to the consumer's spec, not to what our tools emit naturally.**
**Structural fix:** BOM/CPL writers follow the JLC sample
(jlcpcb.com/help/article/how-to-generate-the-bom-and-centroid-file-from-kicad),
grouped designators, verified by a golden-file test. Applies to every board
the app ever exports.

---

## What this review proves about the process

1. **The two-bar doctrine is real.** `fab.ready=true` and "would not work"
   coexisted on one board. Manufacturable ≠ correct, exactly as written.
2. **Expert review catches the class no check can.** Finding 1 is invisible
   to compile/DRC/DFM by construction — every representation inherits the
   same source. One expert, one look, found it. The review-packet loop is
   load-bearing; it stays.
3. **The block doctrine worked even while its assumption was wrong.** The
   risk was documented in the block, scoped to one file, and fixable in one
   place for every board at once. That is what "fix the class" buys.
