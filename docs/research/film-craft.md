# film-craft

## Autonomous TV — Prestige/Oscar craft layer for dramacode

### Framing: what the skill already has vs. the gap
The existing dramacode craft canon (`skills/dramacode/references/`) is tuned for **bingeable vertical microdrama**: hook in 3s (`beat-structure.md`), bond→stakes→gut-punch (`emotional-core.md`), feeling turns every 15-25s, cliffhanger on the peak. That machine makes something *bingeable*. It does not make something *award-caliber*. Studying Oscar Live Action / Animated shorts and the screenwriting canon (Snyder, McKee, Truby, Weiland, Aristotle, the kama-muta researchers), the prestige gap is five specific, buildable things the current skill omits:

1. **An internal arc, not just an external reversal.** The skill plants a *bond* but never a *character flaw / misbelief*. Prestige = a protagonist who wants the wrong thing (WANT) and needs a truth they resist (NEED), built on a Ghost/Wound and a Lie they believe. The whole film is the pressure that forces want→need. This is the single biggest lever.
2. **Subtext.** Microdrama is proudly on-the-nose ("kneeling beats crying" is good, but the *lines* still say the feeling). Prestige dialogue never names the emotion; characters talk *around* the real subject, and action contradicts speech (per Stanislavski/Hemingway iceberg — [Subtext](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtext)). The skill's dialogue register (`beat-structure.md`) has no anti-on-the-nose rule.
3. **Every scene TURNS a value.** McKee/Story Grid: a scene that ends on the same emotional charge (+/–) it began is dead and must be cut. The skill has a *feeling-cadence* rule (time-based) but no *polarity-flip* rule (sign-based). Encoding the sign is what separates "no dead air" from "every scene earns its place."
4. **The right tear-trigger.** The skill's five turns lean toward *loss* (betrayal, irreversible_loss). The science of movie-crying is the opposite: [kama muta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kama_muta) — tears/goosebumps/chest-warmth fire on a **sudden intensification of communal sharing** (a connection suddenly deepens: recognition of being loved, reunion, witnessed sacrifice/moral beauty). We cry at *connection*, usually placed right *after* the wound. The gut-punch model needs this "being-moved" beat as a first-class turn, not just the sad ones.
5. **Shorts END; microdramas cliffhang.** Oscar shorts resolve on a **Final Image** that mirrors the Opening Image and proves the theme; they build on **one central idea**, 2-3 characters, 1-2 locations, enter the story late, and turn once, hard ([NFI short-film craft](https://www.nfi.edu/short-film-ideas/)). The skill has only the cliffhanger ending. Prestige mode needs a different scaffold.

### Verified reference structures to encode
- **Save the Cat 15 beats with exact positions** (out of 110pp, i.e. percentages), for a "prestige short" scaffold: Opening Image p1 (0%), Theme Stated p5 (~5%), Set-Up 1-10, Catalyst p12 (~11%), Debate 12-25, **Break into Two p25 (~22%)**, B-Story p30, Fun & Games 30-55, **Midpoint p55 (50%)**, Bad Guys Close In 55-75, **All Is Lost p75 (~68%)**, Dark Night 75-85, **Break into Three p85 (~77%)**, Finale 85-110, **Final Image p110 (100%)**. Sources: [savethecat.com](https://savethecat.com/beat-sheet/save-the-cat-beat-sheet), [StudioBinder](https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/save-the-cat-beat-sheet/). For a 6-minute short, multiply the % by 360s.
- **Recent Oscar shorts (verified via primary page):** 97th Academy Awards (2025) — Best Live Action Short *I'm Not a Robot* (Victoria Warmerdam), Best Animated Short *In the Shadow of the Cypress* ([97th Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/97th_Academy_Awards)). *I'm Not a Robot* is the textbook prestige-short template worth cloning as a recipe: **one mundane premise pushed to existential dread** (a woman keeps failing CAPTCHA → discovers she is a robot), single question, dark-comic escalation, devastating recognition ending. (98th/2026 short winners could NOT be reliably verified — WebFetch returned data I do not trust; do not encode 2026 titles without re-checking when search budget resets.)
- **Five Commandments / scene turn** (Story Grid, from McKee's *Story*): Inciting Incident → Progressive Complication/turning-point → **Crisis (a best-bad-choice or irreconcilable-goods dilemma)** → Climax → Resolution; and the value-polarity-flip test above. The **Crisis dilemma** is missing from the skill entirely — microdrama does external reversals *to* the hero; prestige demands the hero make an impossible *choice* that reveals character.
- **Aristotle:** the most devastating reversal fuses **peripeteia (reversal of fortune) + anagnorisis (recognition)** on the *same* beat — the discovery that itself flips the situation. The skill already has "recognition" as a turn; the rule to add is *fuse it with the reversal*, don't spend them separately.
