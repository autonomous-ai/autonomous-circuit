# Night log — 2026-08-10 into 08-11

What happened while you were asleep, what works, what does not, and what I would
do next. Written to be read in five minutes.

## The short version

**Autonomous Circuit exists and runs end to end.** `scripts/dev.sh` opens a
chat-left / board-right studio on **:4179**; a board project compiles through a
seven-stage gauntlet into a fab packet with gerbers, a BOM carrying real LCSC
part numbers, pick-and-place, an order walkthrough, and an enclosure brief for
Vibe. Every gate is green: pipeline **145**, viewer **327**, golden blocks **38**,
skills **114**, structural evals **7/7**.

Three things are genuinely notable:

1. **The second substrate went from noise to signal.** KiCad's ERC/DRC on a
   *correct* board reported 359 findings. After giving KiCad the fab's real
   design rules and triaging the measured noise floor, the same board reports
   real defects only — and the survivors independently agree with our own DFM
   gate. That is what an independent check is supposed to feel like.
2. **The viewer is an Altium-shaped tool, not a picture viewer.** Cross-probing
   between schematic and PCB, net masking, layer control, a Board Insight HUD,
   a measure tool, and a Messages panel where a violation flies the canvas to
   the offender — all driven from `circuit.json`, so every pad is a real object.
   Each violation row also has a **Fix** button that writes the request into the
   chat. Altium does not have that one.
3. **The plan loop was broken and is now fixed.** More below — it is the single
   most important thing I touched.

## What I fixed that mattered

**The loop had no approve button.** A headless `claude -p` subprocess is never
given `ExitPlanMode` or `AskUserQuestion`. I watched a real turn: the model
searched for the tools, could not find them, wrote a genuinely excellent plan
to a file, and finished with "say go and I'll build it" — and the app had
nothing to render. Plans and preference questions now travel as fenced blocks
the driver parses, which do not depend on tool availability. Without this the
product does not work at all.

**Gates were set to preferences, not floors.** Three separate times, a check
was blocking at what we would *prefer* rather than what the fab actually
requires, so it flagged perfectly legal boards. Vias were being judged by the
component-through-hole annular rule (which no ordinary via meets). Clearance
blocked at 0.127mm when JLC's floor is 0.10mm. And KiCad was grading boards
against its own stock defaults because the exported board shipped no project
file. Everything now **blocks at the fab's real limit and warns at our
preference**. The skeleton board went from 14 blocking errors to 2, both real.

**Two geometry engines disagree by 4 microns.** The router targets 0.1mm
clearance; KiCad measures the same copper at 0.0958mm. That is three orders of
magnitude below anything a fab can hold, and blocking on it rejected every
board. There is now an explicit, documented 10µm tolerance when handing our
floors to a different engine.

## What I added

- **The expert review panel** you asked for: seven independent lenses (power,
  manufacturability, layout, testability, cost, safety, product fit), each
  scored separately, must-fix notes routed back to the board, and a ship bar a
  board has to earn. Seven narrow reviewers find what one general reviewer does
  not — a single reviewer finds the first problem and stops.
- **The parts catalog** you asked for: JLCPCB's Basic and Preferred libraries
  *are* the list of popular components — already filtered by demand and stock,
  and Basic parts carry no feeder fee. 468 parts mirrored locally and reachable
  from the skill, because the live service takes 47–90 seconds cold and can
  never sit in a design loop. Asking it for a 4.7k returns the exact part our
  blocks already use.
- **Ohm's law.** Nothing structural notices a 10-ohm resistor where 10k belongs;
  such a board passes compile, ERC, DRC, DFM and every image review, then draws
  120mA through a 20mA part. There are now direct checks for LED current, pull-up
  sanity, and regulator heat.
- **The enclosure handoff** — outline, mounting holes, which edge each connector
  sits on and how far its body overhangs, taken from the same geometry that
  produced the gerbers. This is where Circuit and Vibe stop being neighbours.
- **The toolchain canary** — the stack ships ~7 releases a day with no
  changelog, the largest risk the bake-off named. A scheduled job builds every
  golden block on the pinned toolchain and again on the latest, and diffs.
- **A ninth golden block** (`ws2812-chain`), and `BLOCK.md` datasheets for all
  nine.

## What the boards found

Building three real products found more than any amount of reasoning did.
**Six defects, each caught by a board and each fixed the same night:**

| Found by | Defect |
|---|---|
| a live chat turn | the plan loop had no approve button — `ExitPlanMode` does not exist in a headless subprocess |
| harness-puck | the safety gate refused a board for saying *"no mains, ever"* |
| harness-puck | the pipeline reported `COMPILE_ERROR` for boards that compiled fine |
| harness-puck | test points counted as unorderable parts, so they were impossible to add — while the review panel demands them |
| the skeleton | vias judged by the through-hole annular rule; KiCad grading boards against its own defaults |
| the gate suite | two skills' `test_skill.py` collide, breaking CI exactly as CI runs it |

That is the feedback loop earning its keep: none of these were visible from
reading the code.

## What is not done, honestly

- **The three example boards are still iterating.** The **Harness puck** is
  done and committed — a 70mm round board, RP2040 at the centre, an 8-pixel
  RGB ring around the rim, split rails so neither regulator leaves its budget,
  58 placements, ~$57 for five assembled. It is **not fab-ready**, and the two
  errors holding it back are not its fault: they trace to a 0.525mm channel in
  the `usb-c-power` footprint that no legal track fits through, which the
  router uses anyway. Hydrate and Terminal were still in their review rounds.
  A board that says it is not ready is worth more than one that pretends.
- **No board has ever been fabricated.** Every block is compile-verified, none
  is hardware-verified, and the honest gap list in the research doc says what
  no deterministic check catches. Until five real boards come back from JLCPCB
  and get bench-checked, first-pass yield is an unknown, not an estimate.
- **SPICE is scaffolded, not wired.** `circuit-json-to-spice` models 7 of 10
  components on a real board and anonymises nodes to N1..N5, so no rail can be
  identified. Wiring it in would add a check that always finds nothing, which
  implies coverage of exactly the class we are blind to.
- **3D view is a stub.** `three` is not a declared viewer dependency.
- **Agent-level eval does not exist.** Everything measures the pipeline. The
  real product metric is: hand the agent N briefs cold, count how many boards
  reach the ship bar with no human turn. We do not have that number.

## What I would do next, in order

1. **Order five of whichever example clears first.** Roughly $85. Every claim
   about quality is theoretical until a board comes back and works.
2. **Build the agent-level eval.** It is the only number that answers "does this
   work", and its absence is currently disguised by a lot of green test output.
3. **Bring-up firmware generation.** A user cannot scope a board; the
   conversation has to replace the oscilloscope. Generated self-test firmware
   that reports which rails are up and which peripherals answer is what makes a
   dead board diagnosable over chat.
